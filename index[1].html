In-Vehicle Touchscreen Reach Study

The assignment

This tool was built for an HCI course assignment ("AI-Assisted Fitts' Law Experiment & Empirical Analysis"), which asks for:

  1. Ideation & prototyping — define a real-world scenario, then use an AI assistant to build a single-file web app that systematically varies target distance and width, times each reach accurately, and exports the trial data as CSV.
  2. Deployment — host the app publicly on GitHub Pages.
  3. Empirical analysis — act as the participant, record the session, and analyze Movement Time against Index of Difficulty with an academic scatter plot.
  4. Report — describe the scenario and design choices, and state the empirically fitted equation.

Scenario

A driver keeps their hand resting on the steering wheel and their eyes on the road. Periodically they must reach to a center-console touchscreen to tap a climate or media control (the amber HOME button is the wheel rest position; the teal TARGET is the console button). I chose this because in-car touchscreens quietly replaced many physical buttons over the last decade, and the safety tradeoff of a small, distant button for a driver who cannot look down is exactly the kind of question Fitts' Law was built to quantify.

Innovation

A plain point-and-click test misses the fact that driving is a divided-attention task. So on a random ~30% of trials, a road-hazard light flashes at the top of the screen mid-reach, and you must tap Spacebar within 700 ms without abandoning the reach — modeling a driver who also has to react to traffic while touching the console. I added this because it's the single biggest difference between a lab pointing task and a real driving task, and it's cheap to log and compare against later.

Condition matrix

Every A × W combination below is run 3 times (27 trials), order randomized:
